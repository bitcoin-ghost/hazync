extend-spine — incremental genesis-anchored spine (#30)
=======================================================

Host built locally (NON-canonical guest id 72fb6608 — HOME=/home/defenwycke, not the container's
/root). That is fine for a logic test: every receipt below was proved by THIS binary, so the ids are
self-consistent. It does mean these receipts cannot be folded with board receipts, which are be5e0528.

Inputs: blocks 1, 2, 3 proved from archive bundles fetched from the live coordinator
(`/api/witness/<h>`), via `prove-range-bridge` — 79.2s, 80.2s, 80.0s on 16-core CPU.

NEGATIVE — non-adjacent chunk must be refused host-side, before any proving
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
$ host extend-spine range_1.bin range_3.bin bad.bin
thread 'main' panicked at host/src/main.rs:1241:5:
assertion `left == right` failed: chunk is not adjacent: spine ends at 1 so the chunk must start at 2, but starts at 3

  elapsed 0s; no output file written.

This is the point of the host-side checks. The guest checks the seam too (mode 7), but it does so
AFTER a fold — here a ~100-190s CPU fold, ~2s on an L40S — and reports it as a guest panic. Absorption
is the one serial step in the system, so its failures are made cheap and legible.

The second call consumed the OUTPUT of the first. That is the whole point of #30: the spine extends,
it is never re-folded from scratch.

  range_1.bin  224490 B   [1..1]
  spine_2.bin  224506 B   [1..2]

A succinct receipt, so the artifact stays ~224 KB whatever the span. (SNARK-wrapping takes it to
~1.8 KB — measured separately, #21/#22.)

UNRESOLVED — fold cost may not be flat in span
----------------------------------------------
105.9s then 187.9s. The design states fold cost is flat in range length, because a fold verifies two
receipts and checks a seam regardless of span, and that is measured at 2.00 s/fold on an L40S. These
two points go the other way, and n=2 on CPU is not evidence of a trend — the second fold's left
operand is itself a folded receipt, which may cost more to verify as an assumption than a leaf
receipt does.

Not a conclusion, an open question. It matters at scale: if absorption cost grows with spine length,
the ~9,580-fold projection for the whole chain is wrong. Measure on GPU across a long run of
absorptions before relying on the flat-cost assumption.
