# Tip hour 4 — five for five, 2026-09-29

**Every tip block the chain produced in the hour, proved inside the 600 s gate, in an unbroken
chain.** Five blocks, 969,118 → 969,122, none skipped, `tip_chain_gapless: true`.

This is the run the previous night's accounting said was impossible. Tip hour 3 concluded that a
6,269-segment block needed 36 cards and an 8,924-segment one needed ~102; that conclusion came from a
phase table that double-counted execution (hazync#567). Corrected, it predicted 38 cards would do it.
It did.

## The phase breakdown — and it reverses what hour 3 concluded

From the aggregate's own per-block summary, now that it no longer double-counts execution (#567).
`segment phase` is production and proving overlapped; `execution` is INSIDE it and is a floor on it.

**Tip blocks**

| height | segments | segment phase | of which execution | assembly | TOTAL | assembly % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 969,118 | 3,327 | 258.6 s | **157.7 s** | 38.4 s | 296.9 s | 13 % |
| 969,119 | 713 | 73.9 s | **42.7 s** | 20.4 s | 94.3 s | 22 % |
| 969,120 | 1,926 | 158.2 s | **104.4 s** | 30.7 s | 188.9 s | 16 % |
| 969,121 | 2,957 | 228.5 s | **152.2 s** | 32.4 s | 260.9 s | 12 % |

Across the tip blocks: **assembly 14 %, segment phase 86 %, execution 54 % of total wall.**

⛔ **This reverses the conclusion I posted on #567.** Working from hour 3's figures I argued assembly
was the serial phase to attack. For TIP blocks it is 12–22 %, while **execution is 54 %** — and since
the segment phase can never fall below the executor's window, 969,118 carries a hard floor of 157.7 s
no matter how many cards attach. Hour 3's blocks were larger and its numbers were double-counted,
which is how the ordering came out backwards.

**Board blocks invert it**

| height | segments | segment phase | execution | assembly | TOTAL | assembly % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 133,078 | 12 | 62.1 s | 6.7 s | **212.8 s** | 275.0 s | **77 %** |
| 133,082 | 13 | 21.1 s | 6.7 s | **93.5 s** | 114.7 s | **82 %** |
| 133,085 | 2 | 14.1 s | 6.1 s | **40.9 s** | 55.0 s | **74 %** |
| 133,100 | 29 | 28.2 s | 7.6 s | 5.0 s | 33.2 s | 15 % |
| 133,131 | 30 | 18.9 s | 8.1 s | 5.3 s | 24.3 s | 22 % |

⇒ **They are two different problems wearing one name.** A tiny board block is almost all assembly
(the join tree and resolve chain barely shrink with the work), a large tip block is almost all
execution. A single "serial floor" figure averaged over both says nothing useful about either.

⭐ **The three early board blocks are explained.** 212.8 s, 93.5 s and 40.9 s of assembly against
5–12 s later, and the cause is in the same log:

| block | assembly | joins | cards attached | join rtt p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 133,078 | 212.8 s | 12 | **10** | **39,256 ms** |
| 133,082 | 93.5 s | 13 | **7** | 856 ms |
| 133,085 | 40.9 s | 2 | **3** | **39,439 ms** |
| 133,131 | 5.3 s | 30 | 7 | 861 ms |
| 133,150 | 8.4 s | 29 | 18 | 780 ms |

They ran **before the fleet had attached** — 3 to 10 cards of an eventual 38. `[rtt]` measures
dispatch-to-return, so it includes the time a job sat waiting for a free worker: with three cards and
two joins, one job waits out another's whole round trip. The 39-second figures are queueing, not
transport, and assembly inherited them.

⚠ **So `[rtt]` is not a pure transport measurement when the fleet is small.** Anything reading it as
network latency must either check how many cards were attached or exclude the attach window.

✅ **The per-card tail finding survives this.** Recomputed excluding all three early blocks, the p90
spread is **5.5×** with the same best and worst cards (w26 2,957 ms, w13 16,244 ms) — identical to the
figure over all blocks, because those blocks contributed 12, 13 and 2 joins against 200–335 per card.
Checked rather than assumed.

⛔ **The "cards at peak" column in those summaries is wrong** and is omitted above. It read 81, 71, 70,
71 on a 38-card fleet because it counted CONNECTIONS, and a worker is re-armed every block. That also
inflated the printed crossover (133 cards for 969,118 where the honest figure is ~62). Fixed by
counting distinct card names, which #570 makes possible.

## ⛔ The largest finding: ~86 s of every block is spent arming

Spotted by the operator — *"the time between finished folding and starting proving the next block
seems to take ages"*. Measured from the run's own log:

```
09:49:55  proving 133131          <- start
09:50:01  serving block  (+6s)    <- bundle staged, aggregate listening
09:51:27  armed 38 cards (+86s)   <- ████████████████ 86 seconds
09:51:40  verified       (+13s)   <- done
```

`arm_auto_attach` is a serial loop issuing **two** ssh round trips per worker — 37 × 2 = **74
sequential ssh calls**, once **per block**, with no `ControlMaster`, to pods on three continents.
86 s ÷ 74 ≈ 1.16 s, exactly a cold cross-continent handshake.

| block | arming | wall | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 133,120 (board) | 87 s | 95.3 s | **91 %** |
| 133,131 (board) | 86 s | 94.6 s | **91 %** |
| 969,120 (tip) | 86 s | 254.5 s | **34 %** |
| 969,118 (tip) | ~86 s | 368.2 s | ~23 % |

⇒ 37 GPUs idle throughout, at $79/hr. → hazync#584. Tip blocks additionally spend ~110 s staging a
~30 MB bundle onto the aggregate before that.

## ⛔⛔ The whole run as a timeline — 56 % is neither waiting nor proving

The operator asked where the time between one block folding and the next starting to prove goes.
Every block's life is `idle → stage → arm → work`, and summed across all 21 blocks:

| phase | total | share |
|---|---|---|
| idle — genuinely waiting for work to exist | 447 s | 10 % |
| stage — pushing the bundle to the aggregate | 629 s | 14 % |
| **arm — 74 serial ssh round trips** | **1,828 s** | **41 %** |
| work — proving and folding | 1,503 s | 34 % |
| | **4,407 s** | |

**Arming alone is larger than all the proving and folding put together.**

Per block, sorted by time (s):

| block | kind | idle | stage | arm | work | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 133,078 | board | 0 | 7 | 81 | 199 | 287 |
| 969,118 | **tip** | 275 | 120 | 89 | 233 | 442 |
| 969,119 | **tip** | 1 | 49 | 86 | 28 | 163 |
| 133,100 | board | 15 | 6 | 86 | 8 | 100 |
| 133,120 | board | 9 | 6 | 87 | 5 | 98 |
| 969,120 | **tip** | 1 | 110 | 86 | 133 | 329 |
| 133,150 | board | 4 | 6 | 86 | 8 | 100 |
| 969,121 | **tip** | 1 | 164 | 86 | 195 | 445 |
| 969,122 | **tip** | 0 | 89 | 87 | 437 | 613 |

⇒ **`arm` is 86–96 s on every block regardless of size.** On a board block doing 5–18 s of work it is
~85 % of the block. On a tip block it sits on top of a 49–164 s stage, so the pause between one block
finishing and the next actually proving is roughly `idle + stage + arm` ≈ 250 s.

⚠ **`idle` is small and mostly legitimate** — 447 s total, and the two large entries (275 s before
969,118, and the gaps absorbed by board fill) are the chain not having produced a block yet. The run
was not sitting on its hands; it was doing setup.

⚠ **Sorted by TIME, not by height.** Tip heights (969,xxx) and board heights (133,xxx) interleave, so
sorting by height produces nonsense — my first version of this table reported a *negative* gap of
−2,530 s before spotting it.

## ⛔ Board fill can delay the tip by a whole block

| tip bundle appeared | what the fleet did |
|---|---|
| 969,120 — 08:42:16 | `proving 969120` at **08:42:17** — 1 s later ✅ |
| 969,121 — 08:55:29 | `proving 133152` (BOARD) at **08:55:33** — 4 s later ⛔ |
| | tip 969,121 not started until **08:58:40** — **191 s late** |

Tip precedence is not weak in general: 969,122 was taken in the *same second* the fleet freed up, and
133,087 was abandoned 3 s after 969,118's bundle landed. The failure is confined to a ~4 s window
between a bundle landing and the next selection tick. → hazync#585.

⚠ Operating note: `--no-board-fill` avoids this entirely and is the right setting for a measurement
hour. Board fill was enabled here to avoid idling cards at $2.09/hr each. It bought 16 board blocks
and cost 191 s of tip latency — a bad trade when tip latency is the thing being measured.

## ⭐ First per-card join data, and it refutes a prediction

v0.22.1 put `card=` on the `[rtt]` line (hazync#570). The aggregate's `agg-history.log` carries
**9,387 labelled join samples over 12 levels** — the first time a join can be attributed to a card
rather than to a shared egress IP.

**Lever 2 (`HAZYNC_JOIN_LOCAL_MAX`) is not worth enabling on this fleet.** The prediction written
into `join_levels.py` was ~36 s saved; the measured answer is that **no ceiling helps** — every value
is zero or negative:

```
level  pairs    p50      p90       max
    0   4690  3688ms   9208ms   51562ms
    1   2343  3409ms   8007ms   44578ms
    ...
    8     17   498ms    629ms     716ms
   11      2   619ms    957ms     957ms
```

The narrow top levels cost **~500–620 ms**, not the 17.1 s extrapolated from a different run, so
proving them locally (~645 ms of compute) is *slower* than shipping them out. ⛔ The extrapolation was
mine and the data refutes it; that is what the tool was built for.

**The cost is at the other end.** Level 0 has p50 3.7 s against a max of 51.6 s, and per card:

| card | p50 | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| **w13** | 6,934 ms | **16,437 ms** | 40,481 ms |
| w18 | 4,702 ms | 13,207 ms | 39,256 ms |
| **w26** | 1,078 ms | **2,957 ms** | 4,541 ms |

**5.6× spread in p90 across 37 cards.** The fold waits for the slowest peer at every level, so w13
sets the wall for everyone. This is the measurement hazync#550 was blocked on, and it argues for
banking cards by measured tail rather than by count or card model.

## Live-page defects found and fixed during the run

- **The counter fell 2/6 → 1/6 and green grid tiles reverted to orange** whenever board fill started.
  `_newest` was the numerically highest height, which is always the tip block, so a finished tip block
  kept "benefit of the doubt" and un-finished itself. Fixed by ordering on last activity and latching
  `done`. → hazync#582
- **No hour timer**, so `2 / 6` could mean two blocks in six minutes or fifty. Added. → hazync#582
- **The pulse line** from the join tree to the grid was removed on request. → hazync#582
- **Grid cells carry no block number**, so the grid says how many blocks are in each state and never
  which. → hazync#586
- ⚠ **The collector's liveness depends on an external HTTP call.** `chain_facts()` does a synchronous
  `urlopen(timeout=10)` inside the render loop, so an unreachable API freezes the whole feed for up to
  10 s at a time. The API was healthy (5/5 HTTP 200); the laptop's network was saturated by 81 ssh
  streams. Not yet filed.
- ⚠ **`collect.py`'s single-collector lock does not check whether the pid is alive**, so a stale lock
  refuses a legitimate restart and pushes the operator toward `--steal`, the flag that disables the
  check. → hazync#583

## Honest limits

- **One hour, one fleet, one card type.** Nothing here says what a mixed or cheaper fleet does.
- **The 42 s margin on 969,122** is thin, and block sizes vary ~4×. A larger block in this window
  would have been the interesting case and did not arrive.
- **`--clock-from-tip` excludes 13.0 min of waiting and $17.74** from the hour. Billed, not counted —
  stated rather than buried.
- **The per-card tails are one run.** w13 being slowest here does not mean a card model or a site is
  slow; it means that pod was, that evening.
- **Arming was not fixed during the run**, so every number above carries ~86 s that is known to be
  removable. Do not treat these walls as the floor.
