# A Bitcoin block proved in 8 minutes for $1.19 — block 966,256, 2026-09-10

Block **966,256** — 4,741 transactions, 9,079 inputs — was proved end to end in **485.2 seconds
(8.09 minutes)** across **26 rented RTX 4090s** on seven providers' hardware in seven countries, and
the resulting receipt verifies against the canonical guest id `37987b85…`.

The cost of the GPU time was **$1.19**.

## What is verifiable, and what is merely attested

These are different claims and they deserve different weight.

**Cryptographically verifiable — no trust in us required:**

| | |
|---|---|
| the block fixture | `block_966256.json`, sha256 `41271d4f6ffd7048dc01caef62c5f1d223e34dd8737f8975d9c8927220d953a2`, **published before the run** at `https://bitcoinghost.org/hazync/repro/block_966256.json.gz` |
| the prover | the signed **v0.21.0 release** artifact `hazync-host-x86_64-linux-gnu-cuda`, sha256 prefix `24d6b803…`, fetched from GitHub by every pod |
| the guest | `METHOD_ID = 37987b85ec665970ac6c5e8031deb8160ac8ed846f09056c3790b5f78c8bb5dd` |
| the 26 chunk receipts | in `receipts/`, each verified against `METHOD_ID` on the way into the aggregate |

Every card independently checked **both** the block sha256 and the METHOD_ID before it was allowed to
join the fleet; a mismatch aborted that pod rather than letting it contribute.

⚠ **The 8.09 minutes is attested, not proved.** It rests on logs and 1 Hz telemetry. Nothing about a
wall-clock is cryptographic, and we are not going to imply otherwise by putting it next to a digest
and hoping the authority rubs off.

⛔ **The final aggregate receipt was not persisted.** `seg-serve` verifies the receipt in process and
prints its digest, but never writes it to disk (there is no `fs::write` on that path) — so what we
hold is the verified digest in `aggregate/agg.log`, not the receipt. The 26 chunk receipts *are*
kept, and they are the expensive artifact: anyone can re-run the aggregate from them and check the
digest matches. Filed as a defect; a milestone run should end with a file.

✅ **The digest reproduced four times.** Four independent aggregate runs — at 19, 21, 22 and 26
workers, over different physical machines — produced the byte-identical digest above. The receipt is
a function of the block, not of how the work was divided.

## Reproducing it

```sh
curl -fLO https://bitcoinghost.org/hazync/repro/block_966256.json.gz && gunzip block_966256.json.gz
sha256sum block_966256.json     # 41271d4f6ffd7048dc01caef62c5f1d223e34dd8737f8975d9c8927220d953a2

curl -fLO https://github.com/bitcoin-ghost/hazync/releases/download/v0.21.0/hazync-host-x86_64-linux-gnu-cuda
chmod +x hazync-host-x86_64-linux-gnu-cuda
./hazync-host-x86_64-linux-gnu-cuda method-id   # expect 37987b85…

export HAZYNC_LIFTX_HINT=1 HAZYNC_FIELD_BIGINT2=1 HAZYNC_ECMULT_WINDOW=21
export HAZYNC_BLOCK=$PWD/block_966256.json HAZYNC_CHUNKS=26
./hazync-host-x86_64-linux-gnu-cuda prove-chunk <0..25>     # one per card
# then, with all 26 chunk_N.bin in one directory:
HAZYNC_AGG=1 HAZYNC_PORT=9110 ./hazync-host-x86_64-linux-gnu-cuda seg-serve
```

⛔ **On a consumer card you must first remove the CUDA forward-compatibility libraries**
(`mv /usr/local/cuda*/compat /usr/local/cuda*/compat.disabled; ldconfig`). They only work on
datacenter GPUs; on a 4090 CUDA fails with `Error 804: forward compatibility was attempted on non
supported HW` and sppark aborts with *"no CUDA-capable device is detected"* — while `nvidia-smi`
still cheerfully reports the card, so it reads as a code bug rather than an image bug.

## What went wrong on the way

Three earlier attempts failed, and the failures are more useful than the success.

**hazync#147 — a silent wedge.** One card printed its first log line and then sat at **0% GPU with a
live process for 14 minutes**. Nothing was watching, so it cost the entire attempt. The fix is a
watchdog on `prove.log` growth — *not* on GPU utilisation, because the execute phase is CPU-only and
legitimately shows 0% for 30–60 s on a block this size. Grace before the first segment line, a short
stall limit after it.

**hazync#119 — an invalid proof.** On another attempt chunk 15 aborted at segment 69 of 74 with
`verification indicates proof is invalid`, and wrote no receipt. It re-proved cleanly on a different
card. This run used the published v0.21.0 binary, which predates the segment-level retry in #240 —
with that merged, a #119 fault costs one segment instead of a chunk.

**Stale receipts nearly faked the result.** A pod still holding receipts from a previous attempt made
the orchestrator report a 97-segment chunk "complete" in 63 seconds. Because those were *valid
receipts for the same block*, the proof would still have verified — only the timing would have been a
lie. Pods are now cleared **and verified empty**, and the run aborts if any pod fails that check.

**Rented hardware is unreliable at a rate worth planning for.** Of 24 pods rented in the first batch,
8 had non-functional CUDA (`torch.cuda.is_available()` false, and restoring the compat libraries did
not help) and 8 never exposed SSH at all — a **33% yield**. Later batches ran better, but three
Canadian pods went network-dark mid-session while the provider still reported them RUNNING. The
answer is a cheap CUDA probe before the 410 MB binary is pushed, and terminating anything that fails.

⚠ **A crashed prove looks like the fastest card.** Failed runs return in 10–25 s and sort to the top
of a speed ranking. Screening must gate on `rc=0` before it ranks on time, or it selects the broken
machines.

## Cost

**$1.191** of GPU time for the proof itself — 26 cards × $0.34/hr × 485.2 s.

The honest total for the evening, including every wasted rental, failed attempt and idle minute, was
about **$40**. The marginal cost of a proof, once a fleet is warm, is the $1.19.
